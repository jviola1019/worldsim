## 3. Fixes and how each is judged

### F1. City composition (planner, `civilizations/urban.ts`)

**Cause.**
- In industrial eras, the workshop target is `pop / 15000` with no cap, and `isObsolete` only retires workshops above that same count.
- The school score adds `pop / 10000`, so a 480k city "wants" about 48 schools.

**Change.**
- Workshops: in industrial eras, aim for `min(pop / 15000, 3 + 3·(1 − automation))`, and treat workshops above that target as obsolete, so redevelopment replaces them.
- Schools: the population term becomes `min(pop / 10000, 4 + pop / 60000)`.

**Judged by:**
- In a grown modern city, workshop and school districts fall as a share of city districts (test).
- Unemployment does not rise. Round 6 found that fewer industrial districts raised it, so this is checked with F2.

### F2. Modern unemployment (`economy/production.ts`)

**Cause.** Industrial-era jobs come from districts, market services (demand rises with goods income) and informal work. Everyone left over is unemployed. There is no public sector, even though governments in modern economies employ about 15–20% of workers in teaching, health care and administration.

**Change.** Public-service jobs in industrial eras: `pop × min(0.12, 0.02 + 0.05·welfare + 0.25·(education + health budget shares))`. They are filled before market services and count as service work (output and the jobs split). The rate is a rough target from general public-employment figures, not measured data.

**Judged by:**
- mean modern unemployment over 3 seeds plus realistic, against the baseline;
- the GDP identity and ledger invariants still hold in 20 of 20 seeds;
- a counterfactual unit test: the same world with and without public jobs.

### F3. Climate mitigation (`politics/government.ts`, `civilizations/urban.ts`)

**Cause.** The environment policy reacts only to local pollution per person, and only in participatory states. Global warming itself never changes policy. Fossil plants are never retired.

**Change.**
- **Policy follows warming.** Experienced global warming above 1.5 °C sets the environment policy to `basic`, and above 2.5 °C to `strict`, for any nation that knows renewables or pollution control. This happens sooner in participatory and ecological nations, through the existing pollution path.
- **Fossil plants retire.** Under a strict policy, with renewables known and energy not short, fossil plants become obsolete, so redevelopment replaces them, one at a time. `strict` already cuts emissions per unit of output to 35%.

**Judged by:**
- warming at year 1,000 falls on average across the 4 worlds;
- no energy-shortage collapse (food short and GDP checks);
- a unit test that policy responds to the climate anomaly.

### F4. Round-6 leftovers

1. **Ethos signature collision.** `blockSignature` hashes only the ethos length and first letter, so militarist and mercantile collide. It will hash the full key (test).
2. **Fences and streets across map-chunk edges.** Round 7's `markTile` already dirties the chunk next door. A test will show that a building change on a map-chunk edge dirties the neighbour's close-up chunk.
3. **Landmark clipping.** Arcology sprites are 128 detail px tall and stand on a 64-px footprint, so they rise 64 px above their row. The overhang (`DPAD`) is 32, and the renderer draws close-up chunks nearest-first, so a later upper chunk can paint over a landmark's top. The overhang becomes 64 and chunks are drawn top to bottom (tests).
4. **Ruins.** Ruins count as built for planning but as open for drawing. Planning will treat ruins as open: rubble is not a wall (test).

## 4. The guide (`docs/GUIDE.md` and `public/guide/index.html`)

1. **What EPOCH is,** and how to read the code base: a map of `src/` folders, with a Mermaid diagram.
2. **The heartbeat:** ticks, months and years, and what runs when (Mermaid sequence diagram).
3. **The world:** world generation (noise, plates, rivers, climate) and the tile map's typed arrays.
4. **People:** individuals and cohorts, the demography and ledger, settlements.
5. **Nations:** the economy (production, markets, jobs), technology, government, ethos, missions, diplomacy and war, religion and culture, disease, ecology, the future systems.
6. **History:** events, statistics, the feed and album, causes.
7. **Determinism and saves:** the RNG, digests, the save format and validation.
8. **Drawing the world:** the renderer, chunk caches, zone caching, close-up blocks, the effects registry, Reduce motion.
9. **The interface:** Preact screens, panels, input.
10. **The scripts and tools,** each explained: `scripts/`, `tools/`, `benchmarks/`, and the test suites.
11. **Build, CI and deploy:** Vite, Vitest, Playwright, the GitHub Actions workflows and the Pages gate.
12. **Glossary.**

Facts in the guide (file counts, line counts, test counts) are generated by a script (`tools/guide-facts.mjs`), so they cannot drift silently. Images are existing screenshots plus new captures.

**Interactive companion:** `public/guide/index.html` is served with the game at `/guide/`. It is self-contained (no CDN, under the game's CSP) and contains:
- a clickable architecture map (folders and how they depend on each other, from a generated import graph);
- the tick timeline;
- charts drawn from the real round-8 balance runs.
