## A. Device-labelled saves (`persistence/`, `ui/screens/LoadScreen.tsx`, `SettingsScreen.tsx`)

- **The device id.** It is a private random id made once per browser (`crypto.randomUUID`) and kept in `localStorage`. It never leaves the device except inside a file the owner exports.
- **The device name.** It is derived from the user agent (for example "iPhone · Safari" or "Windows · Edge"), and the owner can edit it in Settings. Preferences are validated the same way as the other prefs.
- **Each save records `deviceId` and `deviceName`.** Exported `.epoch` files carry a short device history in `meta.devices` (the last 8 devices that saved them), so an imported save shows where it came from.
- **The load screen groups saves.** "This device" comes first, then saves imported from other devices, each under its device name.
- **Tests:** device id stability, the name parser, a save round-trip that keeps the device history, and the decoder rejecting malformed device fields.

## B. WorldBox-style close zoom (`rendering/`)

- **Readable size.** Above 20 px per tile, people, animals, units and vehicles are drawn at a readable size on screen, about 3–4 sprite pixels per art pixel and clamped, not shrunk to true scale (1/8). They walk on the streets and yards of the close-up blocks.
- **Real people.**
  - In individual-mode settlements (fewer than about 150 people) and in bands, each simulated person is drawn: child, adult or elder, female or male, with a sprite for their job (`person.job`). Tapping one opens that person's inspector.
  - Notable people living in a big city (rulers, generals, prophets) are drawn as themselves, with a marker.
  - Big cities show crowds sampled from their real employment and age data, now denser at close zoom.
- **Animals:** each herd shows up to 6 animals at close zoom, grazing around the herd's position. Monsters are drawn larger.
- **City detail:** chimney smoke over industry and energy districts, flags over civic districts and palaces, and market stalls. All are animated and step once a second under Reduce motion.
- **Tests:** the scale function, position functions that keep people inside streets and plots, real-person selection, and a Reduce-motion still. E2E: zooming in draws people (`data-people`) and a tap on one opens a person.

## C. Warming: clean power and storage (`civilizations/urban.ts`, `economy/production.ts`)

- **Cause.** Round 8 measured grids that are clean but short of power, so electric steel falls back to coal.
- **Change.**
  - Under a basic or strict climate policy, when power is short, solar and wind outrank fossil plants by a wide margin.
  - Grid storage is built as nations learn it: solar and wind output rises with `energyStorage`, which already exists. A storage district is not added; this is the builder's call to avoid new art.
  - When power is short, electric steel takes priority over households.
- **Judged by:** the same 4 worlds as round 8. Warming should fall from the round-8 baseline, with no food shortage and milestones within ±15%.

## D. Remaining unemployment (`economy/production.ts`)

- **Change.** Care work grows with ageing. In industrial eras, elder-care jobs are about 0.08 per person aged 65 and over, taken from the cohorts or individuals.
- **Judged by:** a counterfactual test, and the 4-world mean against round 8.

## E. Follow camera and favourites (`ui/`, `rendering/camera.ts`)

- **Follow.** The inspector of a person, army, band, herd or settlement gets a "Follow" button. The camera keeps that entity centred, and a drag or pinch stops following.
- **Favourites.** A star in the inspector adds the entity to the world's favourites, stored in `meta.favorites` (saved, validated, never read by the simulation). A Favourites list in the menu jumps to each one.
- **Tests:** the favourites save round-trip, and the follow logic (the camera target follows a moving army).
